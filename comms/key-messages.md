# V4AIR academic communication: key messages

Draft, 28.09.2026. This file contains a one-paragraph summary and five key messages distilled from it. The audience/messaging grid is in `key-messages-grid.md`.

Goal and tactic numbers refer to the communication goals section of the plan.

## Summary

> V4AIR, the V4 AI Researchers Meetup, brings early-career researchers and graduate students from the four Visegrad countries to Kostelec Castle near Prague on 28–30 April 2027. The meetup is for anyone who uses or builds AI methods in their research, in any discipline. Over three days, participants meet the people their research needs, and they can apply with unfinished work. The programme is built from applicants' answers. Participation is fully funded for students and researchers from the six partner universities, with support from the International Visegrad Fund. Applications close on 15 January 2027.

## Key message 1

> **V4AIR is for anyone who uses AI in their research, in any discipline.**

**Idea:** the meetup is open to every discipline.

**Goals and audience objectives:**

- Goal 1 (enough applications from ECRs and students across fields), tactic 1: communicate the event as methods-first, for practitioners across fields
- Grid objectives for ECRs and students: the application numbers due by 15.01.2027

**Why it matters to the audience:**

- Tactic 1 addresses researchers who use AI as a tool and may assume an AI meetup is only for people who develop AI. This message tells them the meetup is for them.
- People who develop AI methods are included as well.

**Headlines:**

- Should you apply? If AI is part of your research, yes.
- A biologist training models on field data belongs at V4AIR. So does a historian mining digitized archives.
- Whether AI is your daily tool or your research subject, V4AIR is for you.

## Key message 2

> **You can apply with unfinished work.**

**Idea:** applicants do not need a finished paper or results.

**Goals and audience objectives:**

- Goal 1 (enough applications from ECRs and students across fields)
- Goal 3 (a valuable and positive experience), tactic 5.1: communicate that V4AIR is an interdisciplinary meetup and not an academic conference in the strict sense
- Grid objective for accepted participants: they expect a meetup format

**Why it matters to the audience:**

- The site FAQ answers "Do I need a paper, poster, or finished talk to apply?" with "No".
- Ideas and works in progress are welcome. Projects that failed or got stuck count as full material.
- The Not-Just-Posters session is for students who do not yet have the results or experience for a full talk and want feedback on their project.
- Every attendee contributes in some form. The organisers work out the shape of each contribution with the participant after selection.

**Headlines:**

- You do not need a paper to apply
- Bring the project that got stuck.
- No paper needed. Bring what you are working on now.

## Key message 3

> **At V4AIR you meet the people your research needs.**

**Idea:** participants meet the right people.

**Goals and audience objectives:**

- Goal 1 (enough applications from ECRs and students across fields), tactic 2: communicate V4AIR as a place where ECRs and students meet, open to both groups
- Goal 2 (an ECR-to-student ratio between 3:2 and 4:1), tactics 4.1 and 4.2: messages for each group
- Grid objectives for ECRs and students: the application numbers due by 15.01.2027

**Why it matters to the audience:**

- Early-career researchers look for:
  - collaborators and team members
  - expert consultation
  - a route into industry as a consultant or founder
- Students look for:
  - mentors and research positions
  - feedback on their ideas
  - a route into industry as an employee or founder
- The application form asks what would be most useful at the meetup. The options include:
  - finding collaborators
  - feedback on research direction or results
  - contacts in industry, funding or tech transfer
  - finding talent or expertise for a team
- The organisers use the answers to put applicants in the room with the people they asked for.

**Headlines:**

- "Can't we use AI for that?" Meet someone who knows at V4AIR.
- "I need a master's student who can code." Somewhere in Poland, a master's student needs exactly your project.
- "I need a co-author from outside my field" She's coming to V4AIR this April. Come meet her.
- "I have no idea what I'm doing after my masters" Spend 3 days at a castle finding out.

## Key message 4

> **Participation is fully funded for students and researchers from the six partner universities.**

**Idea:** participation costs partner-university applicants nothing.

**Goals and audience objectives:**

- Goal 1 (enough applications from ECRs and students across fields), tactic 3: communicate participation as funded
- Goal 3 (a valuable and positive experience), tactic 6.1: state before the event what exactly is funded
- Grid objectives: the ECR and student application numbers, and accepted participants knowing what is funded

**Why it matters to the audience:**

- There is no participation fee.
- The funding covers:
  - accommodation at the castle
  - meals for the three days
  - a contribution toward travel, scaled by the distance of the participant's institution
- The International Visegrad Fund provides the funding.
- The six partner universities are:
  - Charles University
  - Czech Technical University in Prague
  - Comenius University Bratislava
  - Eötvös Loránd University
  - University of Wrocław
  - Nicolaus Copernicus University in Toruń

**Headlines:**

- Free and fully funded
- Three days at Kostelec Castle, fully funded.
- Your stay at the castle is covered.

## Key message 5

> **The programme is built from applicants' answers.**

**Idea:** applicants shape the programme.

**Goals and audience objectives:**

- Goal 3 (a valuable and positive experience), tactic 5.2: communicate the event format clearly
- Grid objective for accepted participants: they expect a meetup format

**Why it matters to the audience:**

- The application form asks each applicant:
  - what they work on
  - what they want to learn
  - who they want to meet
- From the answers, the organisers choose:
  - the talks
  - the workshop topics
  - the invited speakers
- Selection is based on fit and mix. A careful application improves an applicant's chances.

**Headlines:**

- The programme adapts to you.
- Tell us what you want to learn. We build the programme around it.
- "I wonder if we could do something else with this" You probably can. Find out at V4AIR.
