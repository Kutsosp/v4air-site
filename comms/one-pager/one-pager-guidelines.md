# One-pagers: guidelines

## Purpose

A one-pager is a single-page document, usually A4 or smaller, that summarizes a project, offer or idea for an external reader (Mendonça; Grammarly). The reader often scans it cold, without a presenter, so the page has to work on its own (Verdigris). Its usual goal is to make the reader seek more information, for example by scheduling a call or visiting a website (Grammarly). Full detail belongs in the follow-up meeting or in linked documents (Visme; Mendonça).

## Before writing

1. Define the goal, such as introducing an offer or securing funding (Formaloo).
2. Define the audience, their needs and their pain points (Formaloo).
3. Write the ask as one clear sentence (AGU).
4. Gather the supporting material: data, testimonials and visuals (Formaloo).
5. Read the recipient's stated priorities first (NSF).

Venture funds expect more business and financial metrics than angel investors (Vestbee).

## Structure

The sources converge on this order, top to bottom:

1. Logo in a top corner, a clear title and a one-sentence deck or ask (Grammarly; AGU). Verdigris caps the deck at about 24 words.
2. Context: the problem and why the reader should care (Mendonça; Grammarly; Vestbee).
3. No more than three main points (AGU). Verdigris uses exactly three callouts with one metric each.
4. Who is behind it, with evidence such as testimonials or statistics (Mendonça; Vestbee; Grammarly).
5. A call to action and contact details, with the call to action as the focal point of the footer (Grammarly; Formaloo).

The AGU template asks for these fields:

1. A clear title.
2. The ask in one clear sentence.
3. Ideas for visuals that a reader with no prior knowledge can follow.
4. A three-sentence summary with no jargon, then three to four supporting statements.
5. The author's contact details.

Investor one-pagers add these sections (Vestbee; Visme):

- Target market and its size.
- Main competitors.
- Traction and milestones.
- Business model and financial overview.
- Fundraising goal.

The SACNAS CareerCon prospectus is a partner example. It lists partner benefits, priced tiers and a partner quote, and closes with one e-mail address.

## Writing rules

- Use the active voice (Grammarly; Mendonça; Formaloo).
- Avoid jargon and spell out acronyms on first use (NSF; Verdigris). A reader with no prior knowledge should understand the main points (AGU).
- Use short headings, short sentences and bullet points (Formaloo; AGU; Mendonça).
- Use specific numbers, each backed by a source, and absolute dates (Verdigris). Avoid vanity metrics (Visme).
- Make the call to action specific. Verdigris rejects "Learn more" and uses actions such as "Schedule a 30-minute walkthrough".

Reading time differs by source. Mendonça says the main points must come across in 20 seconds. Verdigris sizes its seven-item list layout to be scannable in 60 seconds.

Length also differs. Grammarly puts a one-pager at about 250 words. Visme says to use as few words as possible. The other sources set no word count.

## Design rules

- Keep to one page. If content does not fit, remove content and keep the type size (Verdigris).
- Verdigris sets a type floor of 10 pt body, 13 pt callout headlines and 9 pt footer. NSF accepts a page that runs slightly over when the alternative is a font that is too small.
- Build hierarchy with size, color and white space (Mendonça). Keep fonts, colors and formatting consistent, and avoid overcrowding (Formaloo).
- Use simple images or diagrams next to the related text. Avoid charts that need extra explanation (AGU; Formaloo).
- Link to sources, project pages and the website for detail (AGU; Mendonça). AGU recommends superscript reference numbers with the list at the bottom or back.

## Common mistakes

1. Spending half the page on general background (NSF).
2. Burying the core goals in dense text (NSF).
3. Going into technical or experimental detail (NSF; Grammarly).
4. Covering only the research merit and leaving out the wider benefit (NSF).
5. Claiming there are no competitors, which Vestbee calls the most common mistake.
